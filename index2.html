Why is New Media and Net Art So Neat?

Net and new media art is the future of connection. When our entire world is constantly enveloped in digital communication, learning, and consumption, art in and about this world is becoming ever present. I feel that as someone born in 2004, the internet is a landscape that I feel inherently connected with, having been given my first iPad at age nine. I and many others have found fascination with the relationship between user/viewer and screen.

This collection is meant to evoke contemplations on the experience of engaging with the online world and absorbing media.

What do we take away… consciously or otherwise?

How do we connect with each other and technology?

How does our involvement create new meaning?

Why do we love such antisocial connection?

I've found community in video games, message boards, and social media. I've found comfort and learned morals from movies, TV, and music. The intrinsic narcissism that is flared by the internet has contributed to the rise in interactive art pieces enabled by electronics. In the beginning, interactive art created with electronics widely played with viewer/screen relationships. However, as it progressed, electronic art began to center around the nature of influence that the user has.

In art utilizing electronics, I feel that we are adding the context of the present to previously told concepts and stories, making the art more relevant and relatable to current culture. Today, most people in possession of a device deal with some level of addiction to it. In that landscape of hate and addiction, it's necessary to highlight the areas where fine artists aim to promote meaningful messages in an atmosphere of shallow values and judgment.

No longer are we mere observers of the technology in our life, limited to two channels on a TV set. We are the creators and masters of the realm of the internet, capable of shaping the terrain with each of our individual contributions that ultimately make up the whole. I mostly focused on net and new media artists who create pieces based on connection and interaction as I feel that these are the most prevalent discussions when addressing technology. We must think and talk about our relation to the machine, as devices have become practically glued to many of our hands.

Definitions

New Media Art: "Net Art is art created on the internet, specifically for the internet. Artists who create it use computers to generate imagery, exhibit art online, or build programs that produce unique artworks. Since the 1990s, the movement has been considered subversive because it transcends geographic boundaries and defies traditional art norms."

Net Art: "Net Art is art created on the internet, specifically for the internet. Artists who create it use computers to generate imagery, exhibit art online, or build programs that produce unique artworks."

Featured Artists

Bruce Nauman - American conceptual artist who pioneered experimental work utilizing neon, video, and interactive electronic media. His work explores the relationship between art, perception, and technology.

Kit Galloway and Sherrie Rabinowitz - Collaborative artists working under Mobile Image. Their work explored long-distance communication through technology, including projects such as Hole in Space.

Lynn Hershman Leeson - One of the foremost pioneers of interactive digital art, exploring identity, privacy, artificial intelligence, and technology's influence on society.

Porpentine Charity Heartscape - Interactive fiction creator whose hypertext works emphasize identity, speculation, and user participation.

Jeffrey Shaw - A new media pioneer known for immersive and interactive installations, including early augmented reality artworks.

Olia Lialina - Russian net artist whose use of hyperlinks and web-native experiences helped define what internet-based art could become.

Simstalgia Zine - A collaborative online zine centered around communities formed through digital gaming, particularly The Sims, documenting online identity and virtual spaces.
